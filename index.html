Final takeaway

The countries with the most to lose are not always the countries that caused the most warming.

Sinking Nations should lead readers from place, to people, to proof: first locate vulnerable nations on the globe, then read their country stories, then compare exposure against climate responsibility.

Writeup

What have we done so far?

So far, we have built an interactive prototype for the sea-level rise and climate justice visualization. The webpage focuses on the idea that some countries face high climate risk even though they have relatively low responsibility for global carbon emissions. We added a sea-level rise slider and custom number input from 0.5 meters to 2.0 meters, so users can compare how exposure changes under different conditions. There are also filters for Pacific, Caribbean, Indian Ocean, Coastal Asia, Smallest emitters, and Largest emitters, which help users compare vulnerable countries with high-emitting countries. The main visualization uses a globe with country bubbles, and when the user hovers or clicks on a country, the side panel updates with a country story, population, land area, exposure percentage, CO2 share, injustice score, and a topic-related fun fact. There is also a “Risk vs responsibility” chart that compares exposed land with CO2 emissions share, helping communicate the climate injustice pattern more clearly.

What will be most challenging to design, and why?

The most challenging part of our project has been, and will continue to be, working with the interactive visualization itself, especially the globe. Since the globe is the main part of the webpage, it needs to feel smooth, clear, and engaging, but it has caused a lot of bugs during development. One specific challenge is hover and click interaction, where users need to be able to confidently select the country they intend to inspect. We are also trying to make the visualization more engaging while keeping it stable, readable, and easy to understand. The prototype looks strong so far, but improving interaction, labels, and visual clarity will be the most important design challenge.
